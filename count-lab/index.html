How the count works

1

Voters rank up to three candidates, writing 1, 2 and 3 next to their names.

2

All the #1 votes are counted. Anyone with more than half of the ballots still in play wins straight away.

3

If nobody does, the last-placed candidate is knocked out. Their ballots move to each voter's next choice.

4

Repeat until someone passes half. Ballots with no choices left drop out, so the winning line keeps falling.

So a winner needs two things: enough #1 votes to avoid being knocked out early, and enough #2 and #3 votes from other candidates' supporters to finish on top.

    The auto-planner spends your budget in eight steps. At each step it tries every activity and keeps the one that most improves your chance of winning and of reaching the final two. It keeps your current target communities. It takes 10 to 40 seconds.

    The count, step by step

    1. Set informal ballots aside.
    2. Count #1 votes. Anyone with more than half of the live ballots wins.
    3. If no one does, exclude the lowest candidate. Each of their ballots moves to its next marked choice still in the count. If none is left, the ballot is exhausted.
    4. Repeat until someone passes the absolute majority.

    The winning line drops

    Majorityk = ⌊ live ballotsk / 2 ⌋ + 1

    Every exhausted ballot lowers the target. NCD Regional 2022 ended 78,566 to 57,647, so about 136,000 live ballots out of about 216,600 cast. The winner needed roughly a third of the voters, not half.

    Survive, then collect

    P(win) ≈ P(reach final two) × P(win on transfers)

    #1 votes keep you from being excluded early. #2 and #3 votes decide the finish, but you only collect them while you're still in. That's why a wantok fortress alone rarely wins a crowded seat, and preference-building alone fails if you're excluded early.

    Your own #2s are free to trade

    Your supporters' #2 and #3 are only read after you've been excluded. They can't change your own result, so offering them in a preference pact costs you nothing, while the other side's #2s can carry you.

    How the model works

    U = appeal + wantok bonus + suburb bonus + campaign pull + random taste

    Each simulated voter belongs to a community and ranks candidates by this score (a random-utility, Plackett–Luce model). Voters also lean toward candidates based in their own suburb. Your counted votes set your starting support suburb by suburb. Your campaign adds pull on #1 votes and, separately, on #2 and #3, only in the suburbs it reaches. Reach grows with spending but saturates: the first K10,000 in a community does more than the fifth.

    Protecting votes counts too

    Votes you win but lose before counting are gone: names missing from the roll, supporters who can't get to the polling place, informal ballots, and boxes nobody is watching. The Protect the vote activities cut those losses for your supporters only.

    The race and the vote

    A planning tool, not a forecast. Rival candidates are unnamed types built from the shape of each seat's 2022 #1-vote result. Community shares, reach and campaign effects are calibrated guesses. Trust the comparisons between plans more than the absolute percentages.

    Rivals campaign too. Rivals' 2022-level campaigns are built into their starting strength. "Rival campaign intensity" scales how much of your effort gets through.

    Deliberately left out: vote buying, gifts to voters, intimidation, and anything else illegal under the Organic Law. The model also doesn't cover polling deferrals, violence, or disputed boxes beyond the scrutineer effect.

    GE2027 boundaries. A new Motu-Koita Open electorate is planned for 2027, split from Moresby North-West. New maps weren't public as of March 2026, so these profiles use 2022 boundaries.

    Online reach. DataReportal counts about 1.3 million Facebook users in PNG (12% of the population, early 2025), concentrated in towns. Online reach per community here is an NCD estimate you can adjust indirectly by changing digital spend.

    Population figures. The 2024 census counted 756,754 people in NCD. Electorate and suburb populations here split that total by 2022 ballots and the suburb shares, and adults are estimated with the national 18+ share (57%). The census has been criticised for undercounting, including in Port Moresby, so treat these as rough. Your common roll extract is the better source.
